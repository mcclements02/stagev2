Saga 01 Sports × AI Live on the App Store

Sango Sports Lab

One rep on a phone camera in. A scouting report out.

Film a jump, a sprint, a swing or a throw. Sango tracks 17 joints right on the phone, measures the movement, and hands back a graded report with drills for the next session.

  • The AI doesn't get to guess. Motion is measured on the device before any language model sees it. The model explains the numbers. It can't overrule them.
  • What a phone can't see stays blank. Every sport carries a list of things one camera can't judge. Those come back empty instead of invented.
  • Two engines, one answer. The scoring engine exists twice, in Swift on the phone and TypeScript in the cloud. A test suite holds them to the same result.
  • No boilerplate reports. A report that reads like it could be about anyone gets rejected and written again.
Boss fight

The AI coach started giving every athlete the exact same answer. The model was spending almost its whole word budget thinking, leaving about 20 tokens to actually speak. One limit raised, coach cured.

  • 16 sports
  • 17 joints tracked
  • 6 performance pillars
  • iOS + web coach portal

Saga 02 Marketplace In production

Socks. | Sockster

Laundry, dispatched like a ride.

A customer orders a pickup. The request goes out to nearby Socksters, independent drivers who wash, fold and deliver. First to accept gets the job. Two apps, one backend, real money on the line.

  • First tap wins, exactly once. Two drivers can hit Accept in the same instant. Only one of them gets the order.
  • A search that widens itself. Nobody close by? The broadcast grows ring by ring, and the customer signs off before a longer trip costs more.
  • Money that can't be paid twice. A retry or a dropped connection never double-charges a customer or double-pays a driver.
  • One shared vocabulary. Both apps import the same order statuses, after they drifted apart and the driver app labelled laundry already in the car “Ready for pickup.”
Boss fight

A short-range search and a long-range search were returning almost the same drivers. A library setting defaulted to loose matching, so a customer could be asked to pay to widen a search that was already as wide as it got. Found, fixed, and the refund path with it.

  • 2 apps, 1 backend
  • Flutter + Firebase
  • Stripe Connect payouts
  • Seattle area

My part: I wrote the backend from its first commit and have led both apps since 2026.

Saga 03 Private AI In daily use

offEarth

An AI that works with the Wi-Fi off. On purpose.

A chat model running entirely on your iPhone. No account, no server, nothing leaving the device. After the first download it works on a plane, in a tunnel, or anywhere else the internet isn't.

  • It sizes up your phone first. offEarth reads the device's memory and recommends the biggest model that will run cleanly, from 1.1 GB up to 4.7 GB.
  • A governor on the engine. It watches memory, heat and battery mode while you chat, and trims the model's working memory before iOS would shut the app down.
  • A browser whose AI can't phone home. The companion Mac browser, written in Rust, has an assistant that only accepts a local address. Its network code is compiled without the ability to reach the public internet at all.
Boss fight

A model download failed quietly and saved a 15-byte error message as a 4.7 GB brain. Tap send: white screen. Now every model file has to prove what it is before it's allowed to load.

  • 0 servers
  • 0 accounts
  • 3 model sizes
  • Swift · llama.cpp · Rust

Saga 04 Agent collaboration Open source · MIT

cAIrn

A trail marker for AI agents.

Put three AI coding agents on one repo and they will redo, overwrite and strand each other's work. cAIrn is one rule and one command: every commit that touches code also adds a line to a shared ledger. The next agent reads the trail before it starts walking.

  • Built to merge. One row per branch and an append-only log. If two agents collide, the conflict is the signal: keep both entries.
  • Enforced where it counts. A local hook reminds. The pull-request check decides. From the README: “--no-verify exists and agents will find it.”
  • No vendor, no model. One Bash script. Works with any agent from any lab, on macOS, Linux and Windows.
  • Extracted, not invented. It's the protocol that was already running across 13 of my private repos.
Boss fight

On a Mac, asking “does Scripts/ exist?” says yes even when the folder is really scripts/. The installer rewrote files on every run until it learned to ask git which spelling was real.

You add one stone. You don't rearrange the others.

Saga 05 Open-world game Playable on Mac

The Connected Sprawl

A whole city. Twenty-eight days of runway.

An open-world driving game where the real enemy is your burn rate. You're Zarri, a 20-year-old founder with a car, a laptop and 28 days of cash. Every mission is a choice: go Corporate for stability, or Street for fast money and heat. Built in Unreal Engine 5.

  • Simulated, not painted. Traffic lights and AI drivers read the same clock, so a light can never show green while the cars believe it's red. New traffic only ever drives out of a parking garage. Nothing pops into existence on a street.
  • AI agents play it to break it. Agents drive the game through its own cameras. Every bug has to be caught failing by a test before anyone is allowed to fix it.
  • 3,151 cameras on the world. A camera dictionary re-photographs the city so regressions show up as pictures, not as surprises.
  • Every screenshot is evidence. The version is printed on the HUD, and every version maps to an entry in the build ledger.
Boss fight

A review found the game could be won in about 32 seconds: the first investor cheque was bigger than the win condition. Also on the fixed list: a car that fell out of the sky, and a map pin 27 metres out in the lake.

  • ~196k lines of C++
  • 600+ automated tests
  • 9 story missions
  • 9 buildings you can walk into
